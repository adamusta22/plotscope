Planning policy

National policy that applies here, and Oxford's development plan.

The National Planning Policy Framework (2026), published 17 August 2026, separates plan-making from national decision-making policies. Local plan policies that conflict with the national decision-making policies carry very limited weight unless the plan was adopted under the 2026 Framework. Read the NPPF ↗

Permitted development and Article 4Applies to this site

Permitted development rights, such as Class MA (commercial to residential), Class Q (agricultural buildings to dwellings), and HMO conversions, allow some changes without a full application, usually subject to prior approval. An Article 4 direction removes specified rights in a defined area, so a full planning application is needed. Conservation areas and other designated land also restrict several classes.

Key tests

  • Which PD class would apply?
  • Is the site covered by an Article 4 direction or in Article 2(3) land?
  • Does the building meet the class limits (size, vacancy period, date of use)?

Evidence usually needed

  • Article 4 direction schedule
  • Evidence of lawful use and vacancy

When is permission required? (PPG) ↗

Housing delivery, land supply and the presumption

National policy prioritises boosting housing supply. Where a council cannot show a sufficient deliverable housing land supply, or fails the Housing Delivery Test, national decision-making policy tilts decisions in favour of sustainable development. In the 2026 Framework this replaced the former "tilted balance" with new decision-making policies.

Key tests

  • Can the council demonstrate the required housing land supply?
  • What is its latest Housing Delivery Test result?
  • Is the local plan up to date and consistent with the 2026 national decision-making policies?

Evidence usually needed

  • Latest housing land supply position statement
  • Housing Delivery Test measurement
  • Local plan adoption date and review status

Design, density and local character

Good design is a key aspect of sustainable development. Proposals should reflect local design codes and guides, and development that is not well designed should be refused. Councils set design codes, and many have design or residential standards in supplementary planning documents (SPDs).

Key tests

  • Is there a local design code or design SPD?
  • Does the layout meet space, privacy and amenity standards?

Evidence usually needed

  • Design and access statement
  • Local design code / SPD compliance check

National Design Guide ↗

Affordable housing, contributions and viability

Local plans set affordable housing requirements, which typically apply to major development of 10 or more homes. Planning obligations (s106) and CIL fund infrastructure. Viability is tested at plan-making stage, so site-specific viability arguments need robust, publicly available evidence.

Key tests

  • What is the local affordable housing % and threshold?
  • Is CIL charged here, and at what rate?
  • Are there SPD-based contributions (education, open space, highways)?

Evidence usually needed

  • Local plan affordable housing policy
  • CIL charging schedule
  • Planning obligations SPD
  • Viability assessment (if needed)
